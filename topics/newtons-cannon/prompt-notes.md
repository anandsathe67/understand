# Newton's cannon: prompt notes

## Audience verdict

A reasonably smart 10-to-12-year-old alone. One load-bearing step needs an "older readers" box; none is trust-only.

## Feasibility check

| Step | Tools needed | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| A thrown ball and a dropped ball land together | timing, watching | shown |
| Everything falls about 5 m in the first second | measuring in the sim | shown |
| The Earth's surface drops about 5 m per 8 km | measuring in the sim | shown |
| At 8 km a second the fall matches the curve | comparing two numbers | shown |
| Faster gives an oval; past 11 km a second it leaves | trying speeds | shown (why ovals: older box) |
| Gravity fades with distance, by the square | multiplying 60 × 60 | older box; the main text gives the outcome and the sim checks it |
| The Moon is the same cannonball | multiplying, reading the sim | shown |
| Things keep moving unless pulled | switching gravity off | shown |

## Format

Book. One cannon is reused by chapters 5 to 11, so shared state beats scrolling back. Twelve chapters, with a natural stop after chapter 7. Thread number: the reader's farthest shot so far.

## Numbers the page should reproduce (computed, not from memory)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Speed to orbit at ground level | 7.91 km/s, one lap in 84 minutes |
| Leaving speed | 11.2 km/s |
| Surface drop over 8 km | 5.02 m |
| Space Station, 400 km up | 7.67 km/s, 92 minutes, gravity 89% of ground level |
| Moon | 60.3 Earth radii, 1.02 km/s, 27.5 days in this simple model (the real month is 27.3), falls 1.35 mm in a second |
| One-day orbit | about 35,800 km up, 3.07 km/s |

## Dates to check on the built page

Newton's *Treatise of the System of the World* 1728 (he died 1727); apple about 1666; Sputnik 4 October 1957; Apollo 15 hammer and feather 1971.

## Left out on purpose

Kepler's laws, the word "centripetal", rockets and how you actually reach 8 km a second, tides, the Earth going round the Sun.
